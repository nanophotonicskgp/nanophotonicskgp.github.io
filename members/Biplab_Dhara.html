  1. JMCC
    Positional-isomer-dependent halogen-bond topology governing crystal mechanics and optical waveguiding
    Priya Paul, Biplab Dhara, Prantik Dutta, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Materials Chemistry C, Nov 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Deliberate design of molecular crystals with predictable mechanical properties remains an elusive goal in crystal engineering, as even subtle changes in the molecular structure can produce profound differences in collective solid-state behavior. Systematic investigations of structurally related molecular crystals, particularly positional isomers, are very important for elucidating the relationship between the molecular structure, crystal packing, and macroscopic properties. Herein, we analyse positional isomers of 2,5-bis(halophenyl)-1,3,4-oxadiazole (XBPO) crystals, with an emphasis on how halogen substitution and positional isomerism affect their mechanical and optical properties. Changing the halogen substitution from ortho to meta to para positions induces a transition from brittle to plastically deformable after bending and totally elastic behaviour, governed by changes in the topology, geometry, and directionality of intermolecular halogen⋯halogen interactions. The ortho isomers feature type-I halogen⋯halogen and halogen⋯π contacts, whereas the meta and para isomers form isostructural arrangements with bifurcated type-II interactions that generate anisotropic, mechanically compliant planes. Nanoindentation confirms the exceptional softness of p-BrBPO, which exhibits an elastic modulus (E) of 2.76 GPa and a hardness (H) of 0.18 GPa. DFT analyses correlate these interactions with the macroscopic mechanical response. Notably, p-BrBPO retains efficient optical waveguiding, with average optical loss coefficients of 0.12 ± 0.05 dB mm−1 when straight and 0.18 ± 0.05 dB mm−1 with different bending angles. These results demonstrate that positional isomerism can program the interaction topology of molecular crystals and thereby couple mechanical compliance with optical functionality, providing a general design principle for multifunctional flexible crystalline materials.
